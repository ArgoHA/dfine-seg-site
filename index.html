D-FINE-seg is a family of vision models that find objects, outline them, label every pixel of a scene and place keypoints such as human pose, fast enough to run on a robot, a car or a camera.

Astronomy · keypoints

Radio galaxies and their hosts

Jets from a black hole glow in radio (amber). The galaxy that launched them is a faint infrared dot (gray), often far from the brightest radio. The model boxes each radio galaxy, names its type and pins its host: 70.5 host AP vs 48.2, ahead of every published model.

Built for machines that work outside

Off-road vehicles, construction equipment, underwater robots and planetary rovers need perception that is accurate, fast and small enough to run on board. D-FINE-seg trains on your own data, exports to TensorRT, ONNX, OpenVINO and CoreML, and ships with a C++ runtime for embedded GPUs.

Questions · license, comparisons, custom keypoints, training

What is D-FINE-seg?

An open-source framework and model family for real-time object detection, keypoint estimation, instance and semantic segmentation. One codebase, one config setting per task (task: detect | segment | sem_seg | kpts), five model sizes from N to X, from training to export and deployment. The detection core follows D-FINE, a real-time detector from the DETR (detection transformer) family; the segmentation and keypoint heads and the rest of the pipeline are written from scratch.

Can I use it commercially?

Yes. The code and the released COCO weights are Apache-2.0, not AGPL-3.0 like Ultralytics YOLO, and models you train on your own data are yours to ship. Only the Cityscapes semantic segmentation weights are non-commercial, inherited from the dataset.

How does it compare with YOLO26 and RF-DETR?

On Cityscapes semantic segmentation at full 2048×1024, D-FINE-seg-X reaches 84.84 mIoU, the highest of 28 models, and D-FINE-seg-L matches YOLO26x-sem at lower latency. On Cityscapes detection and instance segmentation, the S model beats the larger YOLO26-M and RF-DETR-medium on F1 and is faster. On COCO person keypoints, S scores 65.4 AP vs 62.7 for YOLO26s-pose at the same latency. All end to end with TensorRT FP16 on one RTX 5070 Ti; tables under "All the numbers".

Can it find keypoints other than human pose?

Yes. Each detected object gets K points with a presence and a visibility score, so the model learns whatever points your labels define, like the host galaxy of a radio source in the demo above. Labels can be YOLO-pose or COCO keypoints. The released weights are trained on COCO human pose and are a good starting point.

Does it work with thermal, depth or other extra channels?

Yes. Train on RGB plus one extra channel (thermal, depth or near-infrared); pretrained RGB weights still initialize the model. The "Blinded by headlights" demo reads RGB and thermal together: 93.1 mAP50 on M3FD.

How do I train it on my own data?

pip install dfine-seg, put images and labels in YOLO or COCO format, set the task and model size in the config, train. Export and benchmarking are one command each. See the quick start, the video tutorial or the Colab notebook.
